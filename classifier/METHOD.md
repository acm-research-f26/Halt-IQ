I retrained the linear stopping controller with two Strands feature variants: replacing the binary Qwen critic-approval input with the continuous Strands score, and adding that score as a sixth input while retaining the original five features. I used the existing six-round Qwen trajectories for 150 HotpotQA questions and the unchanged 50/20/80 train/validation/test split, joining the supplied Strands + BGE top-2 scores by exact question ID and answer. All three variants used seeds 0–4, 20 training epochs, groups of eight sampled stopping paths, and the original F1-minus-token-cost reward. Checkpoints were selected using validation reward only, with a fixed 0.5 stopping threshold. The retrained original reproduced all five original checkpoints exactly. On the 80 test questions, all three variants stopped after the first round for every seed, reaching F1 0.3588, EM 0.2125, 1.00 average rounds, and 16.61% of the six-round cached Qwen token total. Thus, adding Strands did not improve this stopping policy under the unchanged training setup. The output is p(stop), not p(correct); token figures exclude unrecorded Strands/reranker overhead, and the experiment is post-hoc trajectory replay rather than a live Strands-feedback loop.
